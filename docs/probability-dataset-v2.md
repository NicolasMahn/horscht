# Probability-target dataset v2

This contract describes a one-million-row training set whose target is either a probability vector over the listed choices or one hard label. The ten rows in [the pilot](../data/generated_train/probability-v2-pilot.jsonl) illustrate the contract only. Keep this pilot outside the accepted one-million-row count and out of mass-training inputs until it is separately approved.

The planned mix is 800,000 soft-target rows and 200,000 hard-label rows. Of the soft rows, 600,000 use `exact_derived` or `explicit_stochastic_process` combined, 100,000 use `empirical_frequency`, and 100,000 use `teacher_estimate`. The 200,000 hard rows may include accepted legacy rows after inexpensive structural and identity checks. No split between the two 600,000-row sources is fixed here.

Each JSONL line is one training example. Keep the existing row envelope fields required by the dataset validator: `id`, `problem_group_id`, `split`, `language`, `context`, `question`, `options`, `topic` (or `domain`), `family` (or another supported operation field), `difficulty`, `explanation`, and `provenance`. Use `schema_version: "probability-v2"`. The model-visible input is `context`, `question`, and `options`; treat IDs, language, explanation, target fields, evidence, and provenance as metadata and exclude them from the prompt. Facts needed to answer a probability question—such as stated branch probabilities or a synthetic sample—belong in the context. Do not put the derived target, its oracle calculation, or teacher rationale in prompt fields.

Every row has nonempty `choice_ids`, parallel to `options`. IDs are unique within a row and stable for a semantic choice across revisions; keep them aligned whenever options are reordered. The implementation can create opaque IDs deterministically from the row ID and normalized option text. Probability-v2 options must be distinct and contain 2–265 choices. `answer_index`, when present, is zero-based and indexes both `options` and `choice_ids`.

The planner uses the versioned [probability-v2 catalog](../data/generated_train/probability-v2-catalog-v1.json) and [cardinality policy](../data/generated_train/probability-v2-cardinality-policy-v1.json). New plans record `choice_count` and `choice_count_policy_version`. Counts are allocated across low (2–4), medium (5–16), and high (17–265) tiers with most assignments in the low tier. Medium and high tiers are limited to task operations and input structures that can define that many meaningful outcomes. The high tier uses selected counts rather than a uniform draw; 265 is available when the task supports it, but is intentionally uncommon. Existing assignment plans keep their recorded count and are never reinterpreted under the newer policy.

New plans also record explicit `answerability_status` and `unanswerable_reason` metadata under the versioned [answerability policy](../data/generated_train/probability-v2-answerability-policy-v1.json). Its configurable default mix is 80% answerable and 20% unanswerable; unanswerable reasons are balanced across `missing_context`, `underdetermined`, `external_fact_unavailable`, and `realized_random_outcome_unknown`. The plan asks the author to include one natural, localized target-language abstention choice. The author writes that option and its `choice_id`; the catalog contains no canned translation. Keep answerability metadata out of `context`, `question`, and other model-visible fields.

On authored rows, `answerability_status` and `unanswerable_reason` are optional together. If present, answerable rows set the reason to `null`; unanswerable rows use one of the four reason codes and identify the abstention option with `abstention_choice_id`. The abstention ID must point to exactly one choice. An answerable target assigns it zero mass (or a hard label points elsewhere); an unanswerable target selects it deterministically as a one-hot soft vector or hard label. Probability values describe outcome frequencies or judgments under the stated target model. They do not mean the question is more or less answerable, and the abstention option does not represent model confidence. Do not describe an unknown answer as a low-probability answer.

When a task has paired context variants, keep both under one `problem_group_id` and the same `split`; give each variant its own row ID. This prevents related variants from crossing train and evaluation splits.

For soft rows, set `supervision_kind` to `soft_distribution`, `distribution_source_kind` to one of the four soft source kinds, and `target_distribution` to one finite JSON number per option. Index `i` in `target_distribution` belongs to `choice_ids[i]` and `options[i]`. Values must be in `[0, 1]` and sum to one within the validator's `1e-9` tolerance. Soft rows have no `answer_index`. Add `distribution_evidence` with the same `choice_ids` in the same order. Keep exact fractions as numerator/denominator objects there; do not rely on rounded decimals as the oracle. Do not repeat a canned vector such as `0.6, 0.3, 0.1` in place of row-specific evidence.

The v2 hard-label form sets `supervision_kind` to `hard_label` and has a valid `answer_index`; it has no `target_distribution`, `distribution_source_kind`, or `distribution_evidence`. Here `supervision_kind` is the hard-label source marker, and the target itself is only `answer_index`. Accepted legacy rows without a v2 schema are classified as hard labels by the summary validator and can count toward the hard quota after its structural checks; preserve their original provenance. Do not create probability vectors for them.

Use these `distribution_source_kind` values for soft rows:

- `exact_derived`: a closed-form target follows from stated assumptions and a complete finite or analytic model. In `distribution_evidence`, include `kind: "exact_derived"`, parallel `choice_ids`, a rational `probabilities` vector, and enough method details to audit the derivation. A zero is appropriate only where the stated model rules out that choice.
- `explicit_stochastic_process`: a named, versioned generator or transition process defines the outcomes. Include `kind: "explicit_stochastic_process"`, parallel IDs, rational probabilities when available, the generator description/version, and all assumptions. The pilot computes path probabilities directly. If a future vector comes from finite simulation, record its seed and trial count and identify it as a simulation estimate rather than an exact process probability.
- `empirical_frequency`: include `kind: "empirical_frequency"`, parallel IDs, integer counts, and positive `sample_size`; counts must sum to that size and the target vector is count divided by size. The pilot uses small, explicitly synthetic samples and says so in the prompt context; zero observed cases mean “not seen in this sample,” not “impossible.” Never describe synthetic observations as real data.
- `teacher_estimate`: the vector is an unverified, uncalibrated judgment, not ground truth or an empirical frequency. `distribution_evidence` must include `model_id`, `model_version`, `estimation_method`, and a concise `rationale` separate from the input. The numeric values are judgment allocations, not confidence scores. Do not substitute an LLM's self-reported confidence for evidence or calibration.

The existing validator also requires nonempty `explanation` and `provenance` fields. Keep explanation metadata out of the model prompt and do not use it to leak the target. The current validator requires provenance to be exactly `Luna-generated; label not independently verified.` The pilot adds `pilot_status: "illustrative_not_mass_training"`; its rows are structurally checkable examples, not accepted production rows.

Structural checks can verify JSONL parsing, required fields, schema and source-kind rules, choice-ID uniqueness, option and vector lengths, answer-index bounds, finite/ranged values, vector sums, evidence/vector agreement, and duplicate identities. They cannot establish that the facts entail a target, that synthetic records match their claim, that a question is unambiguous, or that its language is fluent. Track structural acceptance separately from semantic review. Claim semantic or language verification only after review. The pilot text was reread by its author against its `language` tags, but independent semantic and fluency review is still pending.
