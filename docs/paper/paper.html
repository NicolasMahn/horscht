Horscht: a 265-choice decision model with limited Qwen3-4B adaptation

Nicolas Mahn · Project technical report · 2026-10-09 · Exploratory results

Abstract

Horscht adapts the post-trained Qwen3-4B backbone to return a probability distribution over 2–265 supplied text choices in one forward pass. A normalized last-token representation feeds a linear decision head; the final transformer block and head are trained while earlier backbone weights remain frozen. The current checkpoint continues a prior 128,263-row training run on a 23,006-row mixture of historical replay, engine-verified games, independently reviewed document questions, and public training partitions. A fixed 128-row development subset selects update 1080 from five saved candidates. On a previously observed 17,874-item external suite, the selected checkpoint reaches 50.40% accuracy, compared with 49.14% for its warm start and 51.72% for the original Qwen3-4B immediate-answer label-likelihood reference. The current model reaches 38.63% on MMLU-Pro, 85.40% on five Belebele language variants, and 38.53% on six BBH tasks. These are descriptive results from adaptive benchmark reuse, not a new confirmatory test. A separate 64-row temperature fit is exploratory, and the internal test remains deferred. We report the adaptation contract, data lineage, experiment history, aggregate evidence, and unresolved generalization and redistribution limits.

1. Purpose and scope

The project investigates whether a language model's representation can support a simple decision interface without generating an answer or a reasoning trace. An application supplies context, a question, and candidate options. Horscht returns probabilities and a selected option. The choices can change between requests; output positions refer to the choices in the current request rather than a global label vocabulary. This creates a compact interface for classification, document questions, finite decisions, and tasks with explicit outcome distributions.

The contribution is a documented implementation and experiment record. We do not claim a new transformer architecture, a generally superior decision model, or that one forward pass removes the reasoning demands of a task. The released backbone is Qwen3-4B, not Qwen3.5. An initial small template-based experiment favored post-trained Qwen3: mean development accuracy was 88.125%, versus 84.6875% for Qwen3.5 and 82.1875% for Qwen3 Base, across ten seeds with 64 training and 64 development rows. This explains the local backbone choice; its narrow task scope cannot establish a general ranking. The original experiment record describes this initial experiment.

2. Model and learning objective

The pinned backbone is Qwen/Qwen3-4B at revision 1cfa9a7208912126459214e8b04321603b3df60c. It has 36 transformer blocks. The project loads its language backbone without the vocabulary output head: 4,022,468,096 backbone parameters in the recorded runtime. Only block 35, comprising 100,930,816 parameters, and the 678,665-parameter decision head are trainable. Embeddings, blocks 0–34, and the final backbone normalization remain frozen. The frozen prefix has no autograd graph; the frozen final normalization still propagates gradients into the trainable last block. The adaptation therefore trains 101,609,481 parameters, approximately 2.53% of the deployed backbone-plus-head parameters. The Qwen3 technical report describes the upstream model family; the pinned run manifest records this project's counts.

Inputs use the plain context, question, and numerically labeled options fields, ending in Selected choice:. Targets, explanations, IDs, language tags, provenance, and split metadata are excluded. This is the project formatter, rather than a generated-thinking chat interaction. The last non-padding hidden state is normalized with a non-affine LayerNorm and passed through a learned affine map with 265 outputs. For an input with K choices, logits at positions K and above are masked before softmax. Argmax ties resolve to the lowest valid index. There is no text generation, reinforcement-learning reward, or learned stopping policy. The implementation is in experiment.py.

Writing the normalized representation as h and valid logits as z, the decision distribution is p(j | x) = exp(z_j) / Σ_{i<K} exp(z_i). The per-row objective is cross-entropy −Σ_{j<K} q_j log p_j, where q is either a one-hot hard label or the complete recorded soft target. A target distribution can describe a stipulated stochastic process, an empirical frequency, or a teacher judgment; these meanings are not interchangeable. Agreement with a teacher target is not evidence that the target is true. Accepted probability-sum drift up to 2 × 10⁻⁶ is normalized at the tensor boundary while preserving raw source values.

Choice permutations move option text, stable choice identities, labels, and all target probabilities together. A conservative detector preserves original order for ambiguous letter or relative-position references. A fixed slot head does not guarantee permutation invariance; augmentation and consistency diagnostics address only observed cases. An abstention choice, where present in training data, is a supplied semantic option. There is no reserved universal abstention slot, and softmax confidence alone is not an abstention guarantee.

3. Data provenance and separation

The historical corpus contains 142,629 rows. Its immutable 2026-10-01 snapshot contains 128,263 training, 7,202 development, and 7,164 test rows. Much of this historical material was AI-authored; structural validation and recorded probability evidence must not be mistaken for comprehensive independent semantic or language verification. The probability-target contract distinguishes exact derivations, explicit stochastic processes, empirical frequencies, and unverified teacher estimates. The current continuation does not use the proposed one-million-row target in that document as an achieved dataset size.

The 2026-10-08-v4 continuation snapshot contains 43,723 rows. Related documents, translations, game states, trajectories, parent links, choice-order variants, and historical groups are joined before splitting or sampling. Original split assignments are retained, including historical rows omitted from replay. Previously trained groups cannot become holdouts; conflicting connected groups are excluded. Calibration contains only fresh, unlinked groups. These controls reduce known leakage paths but do not prove semantic independence.

4. Experiment design and execution

The experiment sequence was exploratory. Small frozen-backbone readout tests and a 32/16/16 head-versus-full pilot preceded larger training. An overnight stage preceded the matched current-corpus comparisons. Those stages used different data or exposure and are historical context, not pooled replications. The principal old-corpus comparison used the same 128,263 training rows, 37,756,818 formatted tokens, seed, initial linear-head state, aligned permutations, and one-epoch budget for frozen-backbone head-only, final-block-plus-head, and full-backbone-plus-head arms. Their development-selected checkpoints were evaluated on the same external suite. The last-block warm start was selected at update 8,017. A later option-conditioned attention-head experiment changed the head architecture while retaining final-block adaptation; its result is an architecture experiment, not a proof that every attention readout is inferior. The project record builder links the successive receipts.

The continuation warm-started weights from that last-block checkpoint but reset the optimizer, scheduler, sample cursor, and seeded RNG. Two 128-update pilots compared head learning rates 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻³ with a fixed final-block rate of 10⁻⁵. Development soft cross-entropy selected 10⁻⁴; the main run restarted from the original warm start, rather than pilot-trained weights. Training used Adafactor with factored FP32 second moments, no first-moment accumulator, no weight decay, and update clipping at 1.0. A 3% warmup and cosine decay shared the 1,438-update main budget. Microbatches held one question and gradients accumulated over 16 questions, with a partial final update. The main run completed one pass over 23,006 training rows; this is an additional mixture, not the model's complete lifetime training count. See the protocol and pilot choice.

Execution used an AMD Radeon 8060S Graphics device, PyTorch 2.11.0+rocm10.0.0, Transformers 5.17.0, FP32 parameters, BF16 autocast, and flash attention. Audited recovery revisions resumed from saved optimizer boundaries after resource interruptions. They retained scheduler, RNG, optimizer, and cursor state. Compact training checkpoints reference a checksummed immutable warm-start checkpoint and carry changed tensors; they are not standalone full backbones. The portable export verifies all 387 non-final-block tensors against the pinned upstream backbone cast to FP32, then packages 11 final-block tensors and two head tensors. A CPU inference smoke test passed with an invented input and verified the formatter, normalization, pooling, and mask; GPU export equivalence remains untested. Release restoration and distribution details belong to the model release record. No controlled latency or energy comparison is reported.

The original plan used full development selection over 5,417 rows at updates 0, 360, 720, 1080, and 1438. A recorded deadline amendment changed remaining selection to a shared fixed stratified 128-row subset and calibration to 64 rows. Source quotas and language/token bands were chosen without outcomes. All five saved candidate hashes were bound before external inference. Selection minimized development soft cross-entropy; ties favored earlier updates. Diagnostic argmax agreement did not select the checkpoint. The original full-development and full-calibration plans are not completed claims for this release.

5. Aggregate results

The external suite includes all 11,874 items in the locked MMLU-Pro test slice, 900 items in each of five Belebele variants (German, English, French, Spanish, and Chinese), and 250 items in each of six BBH tasks: three logical-deduction and three shuffled-object-tracking cardinalities. It is not all Belebele languages or all BBH tasks. The composite is a question-count-weighted accuracy over 17,874 rows; it is not an equal-task average or a standard cross-project decision index. Belebele's translated variants share underlying problems, so they must not be treated as independent observations for uncertainty calculations.

6. Related work

Horscht builds on the Qwen3 model family and applies an ordinary affine classification readout to a pretrained representation. Qwen3's upstream report distinguishes reasoning and non-reasoning uses; our decision-head experiment should not be read as a measurement of its generated-reasoning ceiling. Yang et al., Qwen3 Technical Report.

Adafactor supplies the low-memory factored second-moment optimizer used here. Temperature scaling is a standard one-parameter probability adjustment; fitting it to constructed soft targets measures agreement with those targets rather than automatically validating empirical correctness probabilities. Shazeer and Stern, Adafactor; Guo et al., On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks.

MMLU-Pro targets more challenging multi-task language understanding, Belebele measures parallel multilingual reading comprehension, and BBH collects challenging tasks with substantial reasoning demands. Our source slices and direct-decision protocol are narrower than the full benchmark definitions. Wang et al., MMLU-Pro; Bandarkar et al., The Belebele Benchmark; Suzgun et al., Challenging BIG-Bench Tasks.

Community decision models offer alternative interfaces: Kev uses a pointer over supplied options, while ImaJev documents a linear decision readout. Mica and InnerJev document additional adaptation or reasoning-distillation recipes. These are useful methodological references, but none was evaluated locally for this report. Their differing backbones, data, request limits, and harnesses prevent converting published scores into a Horscht head-to-head result or a recommendation to replace the locally selected backbone. Kev model card; ImaJev model card; Mica model card; InnerJev model card. Their details remain author-reported metadata, as recorded in the dated peer review.

7. Limitations and next evidence

Repeated observation of external scores influenced the project trajectory. Even though the latest checkpoint was selected on internal development data and frozen before external inference, the suite as a whole is an adaptively reused research resource. The experiment therefore cannot supply an untouched final-test claim. Pretraining contamination remains unknown, and lexical overlap screening is insufficient to exclude semantic overlap.

The 128-row selection subset and 64-row calibration subset are small relative to the data's languages, context lengths, and choice counts. Large-run results have one training seed, no paired item-transition analysis, and no fresh internal-test aggregate. We report no significance test or binomial interval: translations and related tasks violate simple independence assumptions, and adaptivity would remain unresolved by an interval. The reported changes are descriptive finite-suite differences.

The mixture combines hard labels, constructed probabilities, and teacher estimates with uneven review quality. Source counts alone cannot certify targets or language fluency. Historical row envelopes record split: train throughout; the frozen split sidecars, rather than that legacy field, are authoritative. High-cardinality coverage is sparse, choice-order invariance is not guaranteed, and no published score here establishes universal abstention behavior, image understanding, safety, or suitability for consequential decisions. Long-input execution was checked; long-input answer quality was not established by a dedicated length-controlled study. Latency, throughput, and energy benefits require controlled measurements.

The most informative next result would be one predeclared evaluation on fresh, independently verified task groups after fixing checkpoint and temperature. Report historical and fresh internal cohorts separately; stratify by target source, language, context length, and cardinality; preserve translations as linked groups. Source-removal and same-seed matched-protocol ablations could then test hypotheses about the new mixture. Until that evidence exists, the result is a modest observed continuation gain and a reproducible decision-model artifact with clear limits.
